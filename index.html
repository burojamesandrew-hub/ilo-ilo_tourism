About the Province

Heart of Western Visayas

A province rich in culture, history, and natural wonders

Location

Iloilo Province is located in Western Visayas (Region VI), Philippines. It occupies the southeastern portion of Panay Island and is bordered by Antique to the west, Capiz to the north, and Aklan to the northwest. The Iloilo Strait separates it from Guimaras to the south.

History & Background

One of the oldest cities in the Philippines, Iloilo served as a key colonial center during the Spanish era. Its rich heritage is visible in stunning Baroque churches, ancestral houses, and heritage streets like Calle Real. The province was a thriving center of commerce, textile weaving, and governance for centuries.

Known Attractions

From the pristine shores of Islas de Gigantes and Sicogon Island to the UNESCO-listed Miagao Church, the pine forests of Bucari, the saltwater lagoon of Isla Naburot, and the cultural heritage trail of Calle Real — Iloilo offers experiences for every traveler.

Culture & Traditions

Ilonggos are known for their warm hospitality, rich weaving traditions (hablon), and vibrant street festivals. The province celebrates the Dinagyang Festival, a world-famous cultural and religious event honoring the Santo Niño, drawing thousands of visitors every January.

Why Visit Iloilo

Iloilo offers an unmatched blend of sun-soaked beaches, mountain escapes, 400-year-old churches, and a food scene unlike anywhere else in the country. It's a destination that rewards slow travel — where every meal, street, and sunset tells a story.

Quick Facts

  • Capital: Iloilo City
  • Region: Western Visayas (VI)
  • Municipalities: 42
  • Language: Hiligaynon (Ilonggo)
  • Climate: Tropical, wet & dry seasons
  • Airport: Iloilo International Airport

Festivals & Culture

Experience Iloilo's vibrant cultural spirit

January

Dinagyang Festival

Iloilo City

One of the most spectacular festivals in the Philippines, Dinagyang is a religious and cultural event celebrated every fourth Sunday of January. Tribes dressed in elaborate painted costumes and headdresses perform choreographed dances through the city streets in honor of the Santo Niño. It draws hundreds of thousands of visitors annually and is a multi-awarded tourism event recognized both locally and internationally.

February

Paraw Regatta Festival

Iloilo Strait

Asia's oldest traditional sailboat race, the Paraw Regatta celebrates Iloilo's rich maritime heritage every February. "Paraw" are traditional double-outrigger sailboats indigenous to Panay Island. Crews navigate the Iloilo Strait in a colorful and competitive race. The festival also features cultural performances, food fairs, and heritage events that showcase the province's deep connection to the sea.

April

Semana Santa (Holy Week)

Province-wide

Holy Week in Iloilo is observed with deep religious devotion. Thousands of pilgrims visit the Garin Farm Pilgrimage Resort and historic churches like Miagao, Jaro Cathedral, and Molo Church. Senakulo (Passion Plays), processions, and the Bisita Iglesia (church-hopping) are central traditions reflecting the province's strong Catholic faith and centuries of religious heritage.

May

Flores de Mayo & Santacruzan

Province-wide

Every May, towns across Iloilo celebrate Flores de Mayo — a month-long offering of flowers to the Virgin Mary — culminating in the Santacruzan, an elegant religious-historical pageant. Young women dressed as biblical and historical figures parade through the streets in beautiful gowns, accompanied by their escorts. A uniquely Filipino tradition blending faith, beauty, and community pride.

Taste of Iloilo

Food & Delicacies

Savor the iconic flavors that define Iloilo's culinary identity

Chicken Inasal

The undisputed signature dish of Iloilo. Marinated in calamansi, vinegar, annatto, and native herbs, then char-grilled to smoky perfection. Served with sinamak (spiced vinegar), garlic rice, and chicken oil — a meal that defines Ilonggo cooking.

Tatoy's Manokan, Mang Inasal (original), Molo District restaurants

La Paz Batchoy

Born in the La Paz public market, this hearty noodle soup features pork organs, crushed chicharon, egg noodles, and a rich, deep broth. A bowl of batchoy is more than a meal — it's a cultural institution and a must-try on every visit to Iloilo.

Ted's Oldtimer, Deco's Restaurant, La Paz Market stalls

Pancit Molo

Named after the Molo district, this Filipino wonton soup features pork-and-shrimp filled dumplings in a rich chicken and pork broth, garnished with chicharon and green onions. Its Chinese-inspired origins reflect Iloilo's history as a thriving trading port.

Breakthrough Restaurant, Netong's, Casa Margarita, Molo district

Gigantes Seafood

Islas de Gigantes is famed for its plump, fresh scallops harvested straight from the sea and grilled on the shell with butter and cheese. Alongside scallops, fresh squid, shrimp, and fish caught by local fishermen make this a seafood lover's paradise.

Beachfront eateries in Gigantes Norte & Sur, floating restaurants near the dock

Before You Go

Travel Tips

Everything you need to know for a smooth Iloilo adventure

Best Time to Visit

The best time to visit Iloilo is November to May during the dry season — ideal for island hopping, beach visits, and outdoor activities. January is especially festive with Dinagyang. Avoid June–October due to typhoon season, though cultural sites remain open year-round.

Getting to Iloilo

Iloilo is accessible via Iloilo International Airport (ILO) with direct flights from Manila (1 hour), Cebu, and other major cities. By sea, regular fast ferries and RORO vessels connect Iloilo to Bacolod, Cebu, and Manila. The airport is about 20–30 minutes from Iloilo City.

Getting Around

Within Iloilo City, jeepneys, tricycles, taxis, and Grab are the main transport options. For island destinations like Islas de Gigantes, rent a pump boat or join group tours from Estancia Port. Renting a car or hiring a private van is ideal for day trips across municipalities.

Safety Reminders

Iloilo is generally safe for tourists — keep valuables secure, avoid isolated areas at night, and be mindful of belongings in crowded markets. When island hopping, always check weather forecasts and travel with licensed guides or reputable tour operators. Register with local tourism offices when heading to remote islands.

Budget & Currency

Iloilo is a budget-friendly destination. ATMs are widely available in the city but bring cash when heading to remote islands. Most establishments accept cash; cards are accepted in hotels, malls, and larger restaurants. A daily budget of ₱800–₱2,000 covers accommodations, meals, and transport for budget travelers.

Health & Essentials

Pack sunscreen, insect repellent, and a reusable water bottle — especially for island and nature destinations. Bring motion sickness medication for boat rides. Hospitals are available in Iloilo City; medical facilities in remote areas are limited. Travel insurance and basic medications are highly recommended.
